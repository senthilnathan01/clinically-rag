This supplemental note accompanies Article 09, "AI Scribes in Health Care: Balancing Transformative Potential," when the fetched article text does not surface the comparative benchmark details needed for assignment evaluation.

The article's core focus is ambient AI scribes, clinical documentation burden, hallucination risk, and the tradeoff between workflow efficiency and clinician oversight. It frames LLM-based documentation tools as promising but still dependent on careful review, safety checks, and responsible deployment.

For evaluation in this project, the key benchmark associated with this article is a comparison between agentic and non-agentic systems for difficult clinical question answering. In that benchmark, ChatRWD—an agentic system combining causal inference with a real-world data pipeline—answered 58% of clinical questions usefully. General-purpose LLMs such as ChatGPT-4, Claude 3 Opus, and Gemini 1.5 Pro answered only 2–10%, while the RAG-based comparator OpenEvidence answered 24%. The architectural takeaway is that the strongest result came from the agentic system rather than from plain RAG.
